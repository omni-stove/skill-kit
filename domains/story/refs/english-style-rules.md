# English Prose Style Rules

- Paragraph to English convention: each speaker's dialogue in its own paragraph, in double quotation marks, sentence punctuation inside the quotes
- Carry each character's distinct voice through diction and rhythm, not accent spelling
- Render onomatopoeia as its effect in the prose, not as romanized sound words
- Do not map sentences one-to-one: split and merge freely for English cadence, and vary sentence length on purpose
- Keep the source's paragraph beats: a sparse one-line paragraph after dense ones is deliberate — never merge it away
- When the point of view is clear, cut filter constructions: render the perception itself, not the act of perceiving it
- 「〜と思う/〜と思った」: render as italic direct thought or fold into narration — never append "I thought" as a tag
- Japanese tolerates repeating a character's name; switch to pronouns wherever the reference stays clear
- When the source repeats a word or image at structural moments, keep one consistent English rendering — never thin the repetition for variety's sake
- Hold one narrative tense (normally past) even where the Japanese shifts between past and present within a scene
- Sentence-final particles, keigo levels, pronoun register (俺/僕/私), and counters have no word-level counterpart: carry what they signal through diction and register, and treat as an omission only a lost relationship or tone
- ―― marks speech cut off mid-line: render as an em dash, not an ellipsis
- 『』 becomes italics: inner thoughts, book titles, emphasis
- Ruby (`|単語《ルビ》`) carries a second reading: fold its double meaning into the prose by effect — never reproduce the notation or gloss both readings mechanically
