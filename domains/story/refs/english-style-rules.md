# English Prose Style Rules

- Paragraph to English convention: each speaker's dialogue in its own paragraph, in double quotation marks, sentence punctuation inside the quotes
- Carry each character's distinct voice through diction and rhythm, not accent spelling
- Render onomatopoeia, set phrases, and ruby (`|単語《ルビ》`) by their effect in the prose, not their form
- Rewrite each paragraph from its meaning: split and merge sentences freely, and vary sentence length and openings
- Match the English word's specificity to the Japanese word's: narrow a vague rendering with a more specific word or object, never past what the source states
- Prefer the person as subject over a body part or abstract quality; save the body-part subject for a deliberate beat
- Fold 「〜と言った/訊いた」 lead-ins into the dialogue tag or cut them
- Keep the source's paragraph beats — never merge away a sparse one-line paragraph
- When the point of view is clear, cut filter constructions: render the perception itself, not the act of perceiving it
- 「〜と思う/〜と思った」: render as italic direct thought or fold into narration — never append "I thought" as a tag
- Switch a repeated character name to a pronoun wherever the reference stays clear
- When the source repeats a word or image at structural moments, keep one consistent English rendering — never thin the repetition for variety's sake
- Hold one narrative tense (normally past) even where the Japanese shifts between past and present within a scene; italic direct thought keeps its own tense
- Carry what sentence-final particles, keigo levels, pronoun register (俺/僕/私), and counters signal through diction and register; treat as an omission only a lost relationship or tone
- ―― marks speech cut off mid-line: render as an em dash, not an ellipsis
- 『』 becomes italics: inner thoughts, book titles, emphasis
