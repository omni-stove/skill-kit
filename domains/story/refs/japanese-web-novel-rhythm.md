# Japanese Web Novel Rhythm Rules

- Take beats, tension, and slack only from events, sensation, and the POV's judgment state; where they offer none, stay flat, skipping the alternation and the sparse paragraph below
- Keep at least one unresolved tension open at all times; leave exactly one open at scene end
- Alternate what the POV is sure of with what the POV only supposes, each on a matter the sentence before it leaves untouched and each supposition one events can later reverse
- After 2–3 dense paragraphs, one sparse paragraph: a plain fact, the next question, or a viewpoint-distance shift
- Give the reader each fact once, counting everything they have read — earlier Scenes and earlier chapters included — what any reader knows, and what follows from either: cut whole any narration sentence that hands them neither a new fact nor a judgment that changes what the POV does or expects next, or that speaks of the text's course rather than the situation — a sparse paragraph stays, and so does a pause, a sensation, or a silence at a turn or the peak of the Scene, in a sentence of its own; where two sentences share content, keep one; recall an earlier event only for such a judgment, pointing to it with a noun phrase and spending the sentence on the judgment
- Narrate what is present, what happens, and what a thing is, asserting it outright; say that a thing is absent, did not happen, is not so, or is not quite so only where the reader or the POV expected otherwise, and there let it stand as its own sentence and the last word on the matter
